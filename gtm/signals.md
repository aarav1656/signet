## What the signals say

1. The complaint is specific and repeated by accounts with 65k to 239k followers (rows 2 to 4): a screenshot or a wallet link proves a balance, not that the claimed bot made it. Row 1 counted 0 of 22 viral "200x bot" posts with a checkable wallet.
2. The supply side already refuses to publish everything (row 12): a creator will show a result and will not show every bet, because a public wallet is copied. This is the demand for sealed strategy plus public proof.
3. A copy-trade follower pays for a worse price than the lead (row 8) and cannot audit the lead (rows 9, 10). Signet answers a different question than price: which code is allowed to trade my money, and did the track record come from that same code.
4. Rows 14 and 15 name the two halves Signet binds: proof without a human vouching (14) and proof that the live agent is the one that earned the record (15, overfit concern).

## Method and blind spots

- Engines used: `grokweb ask -m grok-4 -d` (3 queries for asks, copy-trading complaints, Robinhood Chain agents), `chatgpt ask` (2 queries, competitors and creators). `treg call treg.web.search` returned HTTP 401 "invalid token" on 2026-10-24 (not logged in), so no treg results are used here.
- Grok returned about 35 candidate posts across the three asks. Only posts re-read through fxtwitter and matching one of the three asks are listed. Promotional posts by projects selling a fix (TryZephyr, VettedBots, TimmyCrypto78, StratAlchemy) were left out as demand evidence.
- Most posts have low engagement (single digit likes, few views). The two high-engagement rows (2, 4) are complaints about fake PnL in general, not asks for Signet's mechanism.
- Rows 12, 13, 16, 17 sit inside campaigns. Row 12 had 171 replies, many likely campaign replies.
- X keyword search was run through Grok's index, not X's own search, so recall is unknown.
